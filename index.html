Vela

Planning for initiatives that cross many apps and teams.

Vela helps a solution lead take a fuzzy initiative to something teams can build. Through guided sessions with stakeholders it records the goals and constraints, which apps are involved and where their boundaries sit, the contracts between them, the open questions and who can answer them, and the decisions with the options turned down. From there it writes requirements, releases and epics, and hands each app to its team for a technical design and a work breakdown that becomes tickets. Every ticket traces back to the goal it serves.

Open the example dashboardHeron Desk: three apps, five contracts, four epics, as of 12 October 2026. Read the user guideHow the sessions work, what each one produces, and how teams take it from there.

What to look at in the dashboard

  • What needs you: the questions, reviews and decisions waiting on someone, with who can settle each.
  • The map: the apps, the contracts between them, and the flows a request takes. Pick an app and open “Inside this app”.
  • Decisions: what was decided, the options turned down, and why.
  • Build plan: releases, epics, and the stories and tasks one team broke its share into.

About this example

Heron Desk is invented. It was produced by simulated sessions in which AI personas played the stakeholders, so the people, apps and numbers are fictional, and some gaps are left in on purpose: they show how the tool reports what is still open. The dashboard is one self-contained file; nothing on this site sends data anywhere.

Why “Vela”

Vela is the constellation of the Sails. It was once part of Argo Navis, the ship of Jason and the Argonauts, until astronomers split that great constellation into the keel, the stern and the sails. The sails are what carry the expedition into water nobody has charted yet.
