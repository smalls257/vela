Vela

Planning for initiatives that cross many apps and teams.

Vela helps a solution lead take a fuzzy initiative to something teams can build. In guided sessions with stakeholders, the lead records the goals and constraints, the apps involved and where their boundaries sit, the contracts between them, the open questions and who can answer them, and the decisions along with the options turned down. Vela then writes requirements, releases and epics, and hands each app to its team for a technical design and a work breakdown that becomes tickets. Every ticket traces back to the goal it serves.

How it works, in five steps

A solution lead runs a series of guided sessions. Each is one conversation with Claude with one job: open it, work, close it. The sessions run roughly in the order below. The order is a guide, not a track: you skip any stage you already have, and some sessions come back for each app.

Understand. A brain dump (Dump & Diverge) gets every idea and worry out of the lead's head. Discover reads the documents and code that exist and asks the lead to confirm what is there today.

Pick the approach. Shape offers two or three options with their trade-offs. A grill decides between them: a grill is a working session where Vela asks you questions one at a time until a part of the plan is settled. Only a grill can mark something as decided.

Say what is needed and who builds it. Requirements writes what the business needs, with no app named. Boundary splits the work into apps. Allocate says which part of each requirement each app delivers. Releases says what ships when, and Epics shapes the work for each team.

Design the build. Once an app is ready, the team's lead engineer signs off on taking it over (the handoff). Design then works out how the app is built, and Breakdown cuts that into stories and tasks, each one tracing back to what it delivers.

Tickets. Tickets checks the build plan against the planning record (the vault) one last time, then writes the tickets as a list or a CSV for Jira, or asks whether to use the Jira tools you already have. It doesn't create anything without your yes. Each session is a command such as /vela:shape or /vela:tickets.

A plan works best as a tracer bullet: one thin, working path through every app first, then one slice after another. A slice is one thin piece of the product built end to end, so it can ship and be tried on its own; the tracer is the first one. Vela plans this way now: the first release says which journey the tracer covers, and each team's first story is its piece of it. How the tracer-bullet approach works.

The vault: one record everyone reads

Everything the sessions produce lands in one folder of plain Markdown pages, kept in git. The folder holds the goals and constraints, the apps and the contracts between them, the open questions, the decisions, the requirements, the releases and the epics. A checker keeps the pages consistent, and Vela saves a short record of each session too.

So when someone asks why a ticket exists, you can follow the links back to the goal it serves.

Who uses it

The owner is the solution lead. They run the sessions, make the decisions and publish a copy of the vault. Readers open that copy without git. They explore it, ask questions and draft changes privately. A reader's change goes back as a proposal, and the owner accepts, rejects or edits each part. Vela doesn't send anything for you: people move the files by email, Teams or OneDrive.

The app teams take a handoff for their own app. Their lead engineer writes the technical design and breaks the work into stories and tasks, so each story still traces back to the requirement it serves.

What to look at in the dashboard

The dashboard is one offline page built from the vault. The example shows four views.

  • What needs you: the questions, reviews and decisions waiting on someone, with who can settle each.
  • The map: the apps, the contracts between them, and the flows a request takes. Double-click an app to see inside it.
  • Decisions: what was decided, the options turned down, and why.
  • Build plan: releases, epics, and the stories and tasks one team split its part of the work into.

About this example

Heron Desk is invented. Simulated sessions produced it, with AI personas playing the stakeholders, so the people, apps and numbers are fictional. A few links between the epics, the build plan and the goals were added by hand. Some gaps are left in on purpose, to show how Vela reports what is still open. The dashboard is one self-contained file, and nothing on this site sends data anywhere.

Why “Vela”

Vela is the constellation of the Sails. It was once part of Argo Navis, the ship of Jason and the Argonauts, until astronomers split that great constellation into the keel, the stern and the sails. The sails are what carry the expedition into water nobody has charted yet.
