The problem: why plans go wrong

Between one sentence from a boss and the first ticket, five things go wrong, and they usually go wrong together. Vela is built so each one fails in the open instead of in silence.

Six things below.

  1. Vague intent
  2. Decisions nobody recorded
  3. Guesses passed off as facts
  4. Apps that disagree on their edges
  5. Work with no traceable reason
  6. Three beliefs under everything

Vague intent

"A desk helper that tells you what matters each morning" could be a calendar briefing, a list of promises made on client calls, or a reminder service. Each is a different product with different apps behind it, and nobody notices until someone builds one.

In the demo plan the lead wrote it down as it happened: "Honestly not sure what morning they were picturing." That became a recorded question with an owner. Vela's answer: a first session that organizes nothing.

Decisions nobody recorded

Someone agrees in a meeting that the admin view is its own app. Three weeks later a second team assumes it lives inside the main service. The decision was real but left no trace, so nobody can find it, question it or reverse it on purpose. Vela's answer: only a grill decides, and it keeps the why.

Guesses passed off as facts

The lead quotes "71 percent of staff missed a follow-up" from memory, and by page two everyone cites it. The demo plan's goals page says instead: "That's a belief, not a fact, because nobody has read the survey report." Vela's answer: every item has a state, and a fact has a source.

Apps that disagree on their edges

Team A thinks Team B's service never returns an empty answer. Team B thinks "empty" is normal and Team A will cope. Both are reasonable, and the product breaks at the join. Vela's answer: contracts list every answer, and flows are checked.

Where this story comes from

From the owner's philosophy notes (not in the repository): one team assumed an account already existed, the answer came back empty, the code read "empty" as "fine", later work built on it, and nobody owned the logic across teams.

Work with no traceable reason

Six months in, a developer asks why a story exists. If the answer is "someone said so", nobody can safely change it or drop it. Vela's answer: every story traces back to a goal.

Where these five are stated

The repository's own summary lists them: late questions, decisions never written down, open questions with no owner, beliefs treated as facts, work falling between teams, and stale notes (PHILOSOPHY.md, "The problem, briefly"). The remedies are in Certainty and sources, Questions and the ledger, Fact bullets and Epics and traceability.

Three beliefs under everything

The owner's reasoning, which the design follows.

  1. Assumptions are made visible, or they become incidents. Each is written down and checked, or recorded with an owner and a date. "Empty" and "missing" are never the same thing.
  2. Boundaries carry behavior, not just shape. If a contract doesn't say what each answer means, it isn't agreed.
  3. Someone owns the whole. The solution lead holds the logic between teams, and the plan is where it lives.
Where these are stated and enforced

The beliefs are from the philosophy notes. The repository turns them into rules: an assumption must carry an owner or a signpost (ledger rows), contracts list every answer (Fact bullets, pinned by test_cases.py), and the lead holds the whole while teams hold their apps (Handoff, PHILOSOPHY.md).
