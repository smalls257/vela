# PermitPath: what I need

From Odette Kranz, Director of Building and Planning, to the solution lead. 1 October 2026.

Council has funded PermitPath, from Tallis Civic Systems, to take residential permits online. Residential here
means the building code's own sense: houses, townhouses and small buildings of one to four dwelling units. What I
need from it:

- Every residential permit applied for, paid for and tracked online, from 1 June 2027. The downtown counter
  closes to residential walk-ins that day, and Eastgate and Millbrook close at the end of June.
- Plan review done within ten business days of a complete application, against about 24 today.
- Applicants pay online by card. The city keeps the whole fee, and fees stay exactly as the fee schedule sets
  them.
- Budget: 1.2 million dollars over three years, from the permit technology surcharge fund.
- Marisela Quint's team at the Permit Center will run it. Talk to her about anything the counter does today.

I've promised council a launch before the summer.
